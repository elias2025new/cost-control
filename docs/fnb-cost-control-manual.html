Module 1: The Core Philosophy (Why Hotel Cost Control is Different)

In a supermarket, if you buy 100 bottles of shampoo for 200 Birr and sell them for 300 Birr, counting stock is simple: every sale removes exactly 200 Birr from the storeroom.

A hotel kitchen and bar are completely different because food transforms:
You buy a whole beef carcass. The kitchen cuts off bones and fat, slices steaks for Special Tibs, minces meat for Kitfo, cooks it with onions and spiced butter (Niter Kibbeh), serves it on Injera, feeds staff on duty, comps meals to VIPs, and sells dishes across 170 buttons on the CNet cash registers.

The 4 Big Holes Where Hotel Money Disappears
  1. Delivery Tricks & Sneaky Prices: Vendor delivers 47 kg instead of 50 kg, or quietly raises invoice price from 1,400 to 1,500 Birr.
  2. Kitchen Eyeballing (Heavy Hands): Recipe calls for 200g of meat, but cook serves 280g by hand. That wastes 40% of your meat for free!
  3. Unrecorded Trash & Secret Comps: Burnt meat or spoiled milk thrown away without writing it down. If unrecorded, management thinks it was stolen.
  4. Bar Stealing & Free Pouring: Bartenders pouring extra-heavy drinks for tips or taking cash without ringing it on the till.
The Plain English Cost Formula:
• Stock Used Up = Opening Stock + New Deliveries + Transfers In - Transfers Out - Ending Stock
• Real Food Cost = Stock Used Up - Staff Duty Meals - Free VIP Promotions
• Food Cost % = (Real Food Cost ÷ Food Sales on Cash Register) × 100

Golden Rule: Never Mix Food and Drinks Together!
Beverage Cost target is 18% – 22% (very high profit). Food Cost target is 30% – 34% (perishable, high prep). Mixing them hides kitchen theft behind beer sales!

ROLE 2 F&B Cost Controller / Auditor (Daily Leak Hunter)

The Cost Controller is the financial watchdog. They make sure inventory matches money. Here is their step-by-step daily routine:

Step 1: Check Morning Delivery Price Warnings (9:00 AM)

  1. Open Purchases (/purchases).
  2. Review any delivery flagged with a Price Variance Alert.
  3. If a supplier invoiced beef at 1,500 Birr instead of the contracted 1,400 Birr:
    • Reject the invoice or demand a credit note from the supplier.
    • If the GM approved the market increase, click Authorize Price Override.

Step 2: Review and Approve Kitchen & Bar Waste (2:00 PM)

  1. Open Waste (/waste).
  2. Inspect items logged by the Chef or Bartender (burnt tibs, broken beer bottles, spoiled vegetables).
  3. Verify the reason. Click Approve Write-off to deduct the stock from the room and book the loss to the waste expense ledger.

Step 3: Conduct Surprise Blind Spot-Checks (4:00 PM)

  1. Open Physical Counts (/counts) → Click + New Count Sheet.
  2. Select Main Bar, check Blind Count Mode, and print the sheet.
  3. Walk to the bar and count 5 expensive liquor bottles (Johnnie Walker, Gin, Wine).
  4. Enter the counted numbers. If variance exceeds 500 Birr or 5%, a red warning pops up requiring the bartender to explain the shortage.

Step 4: Midnight Night Audit — Upload CNet Sales & Deplete Stock (11:30 PM)

  1. Get the daily sales Excel file exported from CNet POS (cnet_sales_export.xlsx).
  2. Open Sales & Depletions (/sales/eod).
  3. Drag and drop the Excel file. The system checks that row totals equal footer totals (Gross Sales, 15% VAT, 10% Service Charge).
  4. Click: "Explode Recipes & Post Stock Depletions".
  5. The system automatically removes all beef, onions, butter, and beer bottles sold today from the Kitchen and Bar shelves!

Step 5: Audit the Item History Card

If 5 kg of Beef Fillet goes missing, open the Item History Card on the dashboard. It shows every date, delivery, transfer, and sale with the exact staff member responsible.

ROLE 3 Central Storekeeper (Receiving & Warehouse Keeper)

The Storekeeper controls the loading dock and the central warehouse. No food leaves without paperwork!

Step 1: Receiving Morning Deliveries (Physical Scale Rule) (7:30 AM)

  1. Supplier arrives at the loading dock with goods and a delivery paper.
  2. Scale Verification: Place every box and sack on the physical scale. If the paper says 50 kg but the scale says 47 kg, write 47 kg! Never trust the supplier's paper.
  3. Open Purchases (/purchases) → Click + New Delivery (GRN).
  4. Select supplier (e.g. *Addis Abattoir*), enter delivery note number, physically weighed weight, and invoice price.
  5. Click Book Delivery. Warehouse inventory increases immediately at the new moving average price.

Step 2: Checking Reorder Needs (PO Generation) (10:00 AM)

  1. Check Central Store shelves against Par Levels.
  2. If Teff is down to 2 bags (below minimum 4 bags), generate a Purchase Order (PO) to Merkato Grain for 10 bags.

Step 3: Fulfill Kitchen & Bar Requisitions (11:00 AM)

  1. Open Requisitions (/requests).
  2. Look at pending requests submitted by the Chef and Bar Supervisor.
  3. Pick the requested items from warehouse shelves (e.g. 18 kg Beef Fillet, 5 crates of St. George).
  4. Hand goods over to the cook or bar runner, and click Fulfill & Issue Stock.
  5. Central Store stock decreases, Kitchen/Bar stock increases.

Step 4: Warehouse Evening Lockup & Cleanliness (6:00 PM)

  1. Inspect meat walk-in cooler temperature (must be between 0°C and 4°C).
  2. Ensure grains (Teff) are raised on wooden pallets, away from damp floors.
  3. Lock the warehouse doors and return keys to the security office.

ROLE 4 Executive Chef & Kitchen Cooks (Culinary & Portion Control)

The Chef runs food prep, stops heavy-handed over-portioning, cuts whole carcasses, and logs burnt dishes.

Step 1: Morning Cooler Check & 1-Click Requisition (8:30 AM)

  1. Open walk-in cooler and check what ingredients are low.
  2. Log into the system, open Requisitions (/requests), and select Main Kitchen.
  3. Click: ⚡ Auto-Fill from Par Stock. The computer calculates what is low (e.g. 18 kg beef, 45 kg onions).
  4. Chef reviews and clicks Submit Requisition. Goods are dispatched by the storekeeper.

Step 2: Butcher Carcass Yield Tests (10:00 AM)

When the hotel buys a whole 100 kg cow carcass for 80,000 Birr (800 Birr/kg):

  1. Butcher breaks down the carcass on the butcher block.
  2. Weigh the primal cuts: 12 kg Tenderloin, 18 kg Ribeye, 20 kg Sirloin, 30 kg Minced Meat.
  3. Weigh the byproducts: 15 kg Soup Bones (worth 150 ETB/kg = 2,250 ETB) and 5 kg Fat (worth 200 ETB/kg = 1,000 ETB).
  4. Open Production (/production) → Enter weights.
  5. The system subtracts the 3,250 ETB value of bones/fat from the 80,000 Birr carcass, deriving an honest meat cost of 76,750 Birr split across the prime steaks!
  6. Click Save & Execute. The raw carcass is removed and individual prime cuts are added to the kitchen inventory.

Step 3: Execute Batch Prep Cooking (11:30 AM)

  1. When baking 100 pieces of Injera or cooking 5 kg of Spiced Butter (Niter Kibbeh):
  2. Open Production (/production) → Select Injera Batch → Click Produce Batch.
  3. The computer deducts 30 kg of Teff and adds 100 finished Injeras to the kitchen stock at 40.50 Birr each.

Step 4: Logging Kitchen Waste (Immediately When It Happens)

  1. If a cook burns a pan of tibs or drops raw chicken on the floor:
  2. Open Waste (/waste) → Select KITCHEN → Choose Special Tibs → Enter Qty = 2 → Reason = Burnt.
  3. Why this matters: Writing down burnt food proves that nobody stole the meat!

ROLE 5 Bar Manager & Bartenders (Beverage & Bottle Control)

The Bar Manager controls high-margin liquor, wine, and beers, ensuring every drop is accounted for.

Step 1: Daily Drink Requisition (10:30 AM)

  1. Count empty beer crates. Open Requisitions (/requests) → Select Main Bar.
  2. Click ⚡ Auto-Fill from Par Stock (orders 5 crates St. George, 3 crates Ambo, 2 bottles JW Red).
  3. Click Submit and collect the crates from the Central Store.

Step 2: Checking Delivered Bottles & Caps (11:30 AM)

  1. Inspect beer crates: check for cracked bottles, leaking caps, or missing bottles.
  2. Check tax stamps on whiskey and wine bottles.

Step 3: Inter-Outlet Transfers (Bar ↔ Kitchen) (3:00 PM)

  1. If the kitchen needs 1 bottle of white wine for cooking fish, or the bar needs 2 kg of lemons and mint from the kitchen:
  2. Open Transfers (/transfers) → Select From: BAR → To: KITCHEN → 1 bottle Wine.
  3. This ensures the kitchen pays for the cooking wine, not the bar!

Step 4: Logging Spillage & Broken Bottles

  1. If a bottle of beer falls and smashes on the bar floor:
  2. Open Waste (/waste) → Select BAR → Item: RM-STG-BEER → Qty = 1 → Reason: Dropped / Broken.

Step 5: Nightly Closing Bottle Count (11:00 PM)

  1. Count open bottles of expensive spirits (Johnnie Walker Red, Gin, Vodka) using the tenth-ruler method (0.2, 0.5, 0.8 bottle).
  2. Count full unopened bottles in the back cabinet.
  3. Lock the liquor cage and deposit keys in the management safe.

ROLE 6 Purchasing Agent & Buyer (Supplier Contracts & Market Deals)

The Purchasing Agent negotiates wholesale pricing, monitors delivery lead times, and enforces price agreements.

Step 1: Review Warehouse Stock Shortages (8:00 AM)

  1. Review Purchase Orders generated by the Storekeeper for items below minimum safety buffer.
  2. Combine small orders into bulk supplier orders to get wholesale discounts and save delivery fees.

Step 2: Manage Supplier Contract Price Books (/setup/suppliers)

  1. Open Suppliers → Click on a vendor (e.g. *Merkato Grain Traders*).
  2. Review agreed prices:
    • If the supplier offers a seasonal discount on Teff (e.g. dropping from 6,500 to 6,200 ETB/bag), update the contract price book immediately.
    • Set the Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) so the kitchen doesn't order 1 single bottle when the vendor requires full crates.

Step 3: Coordinate On-Time Deliveries

  1. Call Addis Abattoir by 3:00 PM to confirm tomorrow morning's beef delivery by 7:30 AM before the kitchen starts prep.
  2. Ensure delivery notes are clearly printed with purchase order reference numbers.
